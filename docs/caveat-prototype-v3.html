High Liability cap is dangerously low § 4.2
In no event shall either party's aggregate liability […] exceed the amounts paid by Customer to Provider in the three (3) months preceding the event giving rise to the claim.
A 3-month rolling cap is well below market for an MSA of this size. Industry norm sits at 12 months of fees, with a higher multiple (often 2x) reserved for breaches of confidentiality, IP, and indemnification obligations.
↳ Suggested redline
three (3) months twelve (12) months preceding the event giving rise to the claim; provided that the foregoing limitation shall not apply to (i) breach of confidentiality, (ii) indemnification obligations, or (iii) gross negligence or willful misconduct, for which liability shall be capped at two (2) times the foregoing amount.
High One-way indemnification § 5.1
Customer shall defend, indemnify and hold harmless Provider […] from and against any and all claims […] arising out of or related to Customer's use of the Services.
Indemnification flows only one direction — Customer protects Provider, but Provider does not reciprocate. Standard practice is mutual indemnification at minimum, with Provider specifically indemnifying Customer for third-party IP infringement claims.
Medium Termination forfeits prepaid amounts § 7.3
Upon termination, all amounts paid by Customer shall be non-refundable, regardless of the reason for termination.
Combined with Provider's unilateral right to terminate for convenience on 30 days' notice (same section), this clause permits Provider to keep prepaid fees with no service obligation. Recommend pro-rata refund on Provider-initiated termination.
Missing No data processing addendum —
The contract does not contemplate a Data Processing Addendum (DPA), even though §3.4 references Customer Data being processed by Provider's systems. Required if either party operates under GDPR, CCPA, or analogous regimes.

Carter & Voss LLP · Memo
Re: Acme Master Services Agreement

What this contract is

This is a Master Services Agreement that governs your ongoing relationship with Acme. It sets the rules for how you'll receive their services, how much you'll pay, and what happens if things go wrong.

What you're committing to

  • Pay Acme on time, in advance, for all services.
  • Cover Acme's legal costs if anyone sues them over how you use their service — even if Acme is partly at fault.
  • Resolve any disputes in Delaware courts.

The three biggest risks

  • If Acme makes a mistake that costs you money, they'll only owe you up to three months of fees. That's unusually low.
  • Acme can cancel on you with 30 days' notice and keep everything you've already paid, without giving back the unused portion.
  • If something goes wrong with data privacy, the contract has no protections in place — it doesn't even acknowledge data laws like GDPR or CCPA.
Recommendation
Do not sign as-is. Negotiate the three items above before executing.

Memorandum of Review
I. Summary

The proposed Master Services Agreement ("MSA") presented by Acme Inc. departs from prevailing market practice in three material respects, each of which exposes Beacon Health to disproportionate risk. We recommend that the Agreement not be executed in its current form and that the items below be negotiated as conditions to signing.

II. Material Findings

A. Limitation of Liability (§ 4.2). The mutual aggregate liability cap is set at three months of fees paid:

In no event shall either party's aggregate liability […] exceed the amounts paid by Customer to Provider in the three (3) months preceding the event giving rise to the claim.

This cap is meaningfully below the twelve-month standard observed in comparable MSAs. Recommend revision with carve-outs for confidentiality, indemnification, and willful misconduct.

B. Indemnification (§ 5.1). Indemnification obligations run only from Customer to Provider. We recommend conversion to a mutual structure with a Provider-side IP infringement indemnity.
